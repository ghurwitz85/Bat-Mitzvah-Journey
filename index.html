Hebrew reading tip

Hebrew is read from right to left. Vowel marks (nikud) help you pronounce words while learning; Torah scrolls themselves normally have no vowel or trope marks. Build confidence reading with vowels first, then follow your teacher's steps toward the assigned scroll text.

Torah detective

Read like a thoughtful teacher

When you have your assigned passage, investigate it with these five questions. You do not need to agree with every interpretation; careful questions are part of Jewish learning.

1
What happens?

Retell the passage in 2–3 sentences without looking.

2
Which words matter?

Choose 2–4 Hebrew words. Learn their meaning and notice repeated words or surprising choices.

3
What is puzzling?

Write one honest question, tension, or detail that you do not understand.

4
What have Jewish readers said?

Compare at least one commentary or interpretation. Keep track of whose idea is whose.

5
What does it ask of me?

Connect the passage to a value, a real-life choice, your community, or the kind of person you want to become.

My first big question

D’var Torah starter

A short teaching often has a simple shape:

  1. Introduce the passage and its context.
  2. Share one question or insight.
  3. Explain a commentary or another perspective.
  4. Connect the text to your life or community.
  5. End with what you hope listeners will remember.

Your own ideas should lead. Use sources respectfully and credit interpretations you borrow.

Vocabulary

Know the ceremony words

Aliyah — being called up for Torah honors; also the term for an individual Torah-reading section.

Haftarah — a reading from the Prophets.

Trope / te’amim — cantillation marks that guide melody, stress, and phrasing.

D’var Torah — a teaching or interpretation based on Torah.

Mitzvah — a commandment; commonly also used for a meaningful Jewish obligation or good deed.

Trope practice

Learn in the right order

  1. Read the Hebrew words clearly without melody.
  2. Mark phrase boundaries and punctuation.
  3. Practice one short phrase at a time with the assigned recording.
  4. Repeat difficult spots, not only the whole passage.
  5. Gradually practice without vowels/trope marks if your teacher asks.
